### The Metric Most Shops Ignore

Ask any garage owner about their shop's revenue, and they'll have a number. Ask about their technician efficiency, and you'll usually get silence.

Technician efficiency is the most overlooked metric in shop management—and it has more impact on your bottom line than almost anything else.

### What Is Technician Efficiency?

Technician efficiency measures how much of a tech's clocked time generates revenue.

**Efficiency = Billed Hours ÷ Clocked Hours × 100**

Example:
- Tech clocks in for 8 hours
- Tech bills out 6.5 hours of work
- Efficiency = 6.5 ÷ 8 = **81%**

An 81% efficiency rate is generally considered good. But here's where it gets interesting: the difference between 70% and 85% efficiency can mean thousands of dollars per month.

### The Math That Changes Everything

Let's look at two technicians with different efficiency rates:

| Metric | Tech A (70%) | Tech B (85%) |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| Clocked hours/week | 40 | 40 |
| Billed hours/week | 28 | 34 |
| Hourly labor rate | $120 | $120 |
| Weekly revenue | $3,360 | $4,080 |
| Monthly revenue | $13,440 | $16,320 |

**The gap: $2,880/month or $34,560/year—from the same payroll cost.**

That's not a slight difference. That's a new lift or a vacation for the owner.

### Where Does Time Go?

When efficiency is low, the lost hours fall into a few categories:

#### 1. Waiting
- Waiting for parts
- Waiting for diagnosis
- Waiting for bay assignment
- Waiting for customer approval

#### 2. Non-billable Tasks
- Moving cars
- Cleaning workspace
- Finding tools
- Walking to the front desk

#### 3. Rework
- Comebacks that require unpaid labor
- Jobs that take longer than estimated

#### 4. Administrative Time
- Clocking in/out
- Writing notes
- Reviewing job assignments

### Why Tracking Matters

You can't fix what you can't measure.

Without tracking efficiency:
- You don't know who your top performers are
- You don't know where time is being lost
- You can't compare performance over time
- You're guessing at capacity

With tracking:
- You identify training opportunities
- You spot workflow bottlenecks
- You make better hiring decisions
- You set realistic goals

### Setting Realistic Benchmarks

Industry benchmarks for technician efficiency:

| Rating | Efficiency | Notes |
|--------|------------|-------|
| Excellent | 90%+ | Rare, usually A-techs on routine work |
| Good | 80-89% | Solid, consistent performers |
| Average | 70-79% | Room for improvement |
| Concerning | Below 70% | Investigate causes |

**Note:** Efficiency varies by job type. Complex diagnostics naturally have lower efficiency than routine services.

### Common Causes of Low Efficiency

#### Shop Workflow Issues
- Poor job scheduling
- Parts not ready when tech is
- Customers approval delays

#### Training Gaps
- Tech unfamiliar with certain repairs
- Lack of diagnostic skills
- Needing help from senior techs

#### Equipment Problems
- Broken or missing tools
- Shared equipment creating bottlenecks
- Outdated diagnostic equipment

#### Motivation Issues
- Unclear expectations
- No visibility into their own performance
- Lack of recognition for good work

### How to Track Efficiency

#### Manual Method
- Techs write start/stop times per job
- Compare to hours billed on invoice
- Calculate weekly efficiency per tech

Works, but requires discipline and creates paperwork.

#### Digital Method
- Use shop management software with time logging
- Automatic calculation of efficiency
- Real-time visibility for owners and techs

Less friction, more accurate, easier to sustain.

### Having the Efficiency Conversation

Sharing efficiency data with technicians can be tricky. Handle it well:

**Do:**
- Share individual data privately
- Focus on improvement, not blame
- Highlight what's causing the lost time
- Set collaborative goals

**Don't:**
- Post rankings publicly (creates resentment)
- Punish without understanding causes
- Ignore systemic issues that affect everyone

### The Goal Is Progress, Not Perfection

You're not trying to squeeze every second out of your techs. You're trying to:

1. **Identify waste** in your workflow
2. **Remove obstacles** that slow people down
3. **Recognize** your top performers
4. **Support** techs who are struggling

A 5% improvement in average efficiency translates directly to revenue growth with zero increase in labor cost.

### Start Tracking This Week

Here's a simple action plan:

1. **Day 1-2:** Have techs log their start/stop times per job
2. **Day 3-5:** Calculate billed hours vs. clocked hours
3. **Week 2:** Review the data; identify patterns
4. **Week 3:** Address one workflow bottleneck
5. **Ongoing:** Track efficiency weekly; discuss monthly

Within a month, you'll have insights you've never had before.

### Your Techs Are Your Business

Every shop's #1 expense is labor. Every shop's #1 asset is skilled technicians.

Efficiency tracking isn't about squeezing more blood from the stone. It's about understanding how your most valuable resource is being used—and making sure nothing is being wasted.

**The shops that win are the ones that know their numbers. Start with efficiency.**
